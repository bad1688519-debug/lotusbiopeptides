CATALOG BUYER / DOCUMENT REVIEW

Six checks before a quote.
One clear specification.

Record the answer to each question against the same catalog item and proposed supply batch. This keeps a comparison of offers and documents consistent.

01

Read purity in context

Ask which analytical method produced the reported purity result and whether the chromatogram is available. A purity percentage and the amount of material in a vial describe different things; record each requirement separately.

Method and reported result
02

Confirm material identity

Copy the catalog name and product link into your inquiry. For the relevant material, confirm the sequence, modifications or other identity details and ask which identity records, including mass spectrometry where available, match that specification.

Exact catalog specification
03

Specify the vial format

State the required amount per vial, vial count and presentation. Ask for storage guidance for that particular material and clarify whether the quoted specification concerns total contents or a separately measured component.

Amount and presentation
04

Identify the test scope

List the results your review requires. Ask whether each record is internal or from an outside laboratory and which tests it covers. Use the testing-document guide to prepare questions about the issuing laboratory and report reference.

Issuer and tests covered
05

Match the batch reference

Compare the product name, batch identifier and report date with the proposed supply. If a catalog report is an example or belongs to an earlier batch, request clarification before treating it as evidence for the batch in your quote.

Report-to-batch match
06

Define labels and packaging

Specify the label text, vial count per box and destination. Review packaging options and identify any artwork or batch-reference fields you need before asking for preparation timing and shipping options.

A complete packaging brief

Available records vary by material and batch. A missing document or unclear reference is a question to resolve in the inquiry, rather than an assumption to carry into your comparison.
